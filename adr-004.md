# ADR-004 — Registration gate, probability bounds and the Resolver clock

Oct 3, 2026 · @Anderson

## Status

In force from its chain record. It applies forward only.

It applies to calls registered after its chain record. Calls registered earlier keep the rules in force when they were registered.

It amends, by addition, ADR-000 rules 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8, and the wait rule of ADR-001. Neither document is edited. For the calls it covers, it replaces the operating rule in 2.9 that nothing is appended to the chain without the owner's word.

## Context

Three gaps in the rules can decide a score for reasons unrelated to the forecast.

**The owner gate.** ADR-000 rule 5 says no person registers, edits, resolves, withdraws or scores a call. The operating rule in 2.9 requires the owner's word, after seeing the record text, before any chain append. Seeing p and b, the owner can select which forecasts enter the Record, and nothing records the calls he declines. Read literally, verdicts, withdrawals and the 23:59 PT anchor also wait for a person, against rules 7 and 11.

**Unbounded probabilities.** Rule 8 scores ln(q) or ln(1 − q) and sets no limit on p or b. A reference class with zero occurrences gives b = 0; if the event happens, score(b) = ln(0) and the result is +∞. A registered p of 0 or 1 on the wrong side gives −∞. One such call decides every sum, including the 50-call test of rule 10. A p equal to b scores exactly 0, which rule 8 labels neither RESOLVED nor MISSED.

**The Resolver clock.** ADR-001 makes the Resolver wait while a model family is unavailable. Rule 7 gives 72 hours, a rerun 7 days later, then unresolvable, which rule 8 scores as MISSED. Neither says whether a wait stops the clock, so an outage or an exhausted spend gate can become a MISSED. Neither says whether an attempt may be restarted, which would allow readings to be re-rolled.

## Decision A: the registration gate

The owner keeps one power: to decline, blind to the forecast, a call that passed admission. Every decline is on the chain.

1. **One person decision.** The owner (Anderson Criativo) may decline a call that has passed every admission check. He cannot edit a call, change p or b, register a call that failed a check, or act on any record other than a registration. This is the only person decision in a call's life.
2. **Blind presentation.** The owner sees the claim, resolution rule, deadline, named sources, exclusions and base-rate type. He does not see p, b, the base-rate derivation, the edge, the Forecaster's readings or its reasoning.
3. **Decision window.** The owner has 24 hours from presentation to register or decline. No word within 24 hours is a decline with category NO\_RESPONSE, appended by software. Time-dependent admission checks, including the 1-to-365-day deadline check, are re-run at the registration instant.
4. **Declines are chain records.** Each decline carries:
   - the SHA-256 of the full draft as canonical JSON, including p and b;
   - the presentation time and the decision time, in UTC to the second;
   - one category: LEGAL, SCOPE, DUPLICATE, NO\_RESPONSE or OTHER;
   - one sentence of reason, except under NO\_RESPONSE.
5. **Declines are published.** The full draft, with p and b, is published with its decline. Under LEGAL, only the hash and the category are published.
6. **A decline is not a call.** It carries no verdict and no score, is never resolved, and never adds to n. The Record shows the count of declines next to the count of scored calls.
7. **Everything else runs without a person.** Verdicts, withdrawals, live updates, evidence entries and anchors are appended by software on their own schedule. None waits for anyone's word.

## Decision B: bounds and precision

p and b are registered in steps of 0.01 from 0.01 to 0.99, so every result is finite, nonzero and reproducible.

1. **Form.** p and b are decimals with two places, from 0.01 to 0.99 inclusive.
2. **Derivation.** Software rounds each computed value to two places, halves away from zero. It then replaces 0.00 with 0.01 and 1.00 with 0.99. The registration record carries the computed value and the registered value; only the registered value is scored.
3. **Equal values.** A call whose registered p equals its registered b fails admission.
4. **Updates.** Live probability updates (rule 6) use the same form.
5. **Results.** Each result is computed by rule 8 from the registered values, with the natural logarithm. It is published rounded to four decimal places, halves away from zero.
6. **Sums.** Every sum, average and threshold in the Record, including the tests of rule 10, is computed from the published per-call results. Anyone can recompute it from the public record.
7. **What follows.** Every result lies between −ln 99 and +ln 99, about −4.5951 and +4.5951. The smallest possible size of a result is ln(0.99/0.98), about 0.0102. No result rounds to zero, so every scored call is RESOLVED or MISSED.

## Decision C: the Resolver clock

A wait pauses the clock for up to 14 days per call; it never cancels the score for having no verdict.

1. **Evidence first.** For each attempt, the named sources are captured as ADR-001 requires (text, SHA-256, UTC capture time, public archive link). Capture happens inside the attempt's window as first scheduled, without waiting for the readers. An attempt that runs later reads the text captured in its window, never text captured afterwards.
2. **Windows.** Attempt 1's window opens at the deadline and lasts 72 hours. Attempt 2's window opens 7 days after attempt 1 ends and lasts 72 hours. An attempt ends when its readings are complete or its window closes, whichever comes first.
3. **Unavailability.** A family is unavailable while its readings fail (provider error, timeout, rate limit) or the spend gate refuses them. A wait starts at the first failure and ends at the next successful reading from that family. Each failure is logged with its UTC time and error text.
4. **Waits extend the window.** A wait extends the window it falls in by its length, in wall-clock time; overlapping waits count once. The total extension for one call, across both windows, is at most 14 days.
5. **No restarts.** An attempt starts once and is never restarted or repeated. If a family becomes unavailable mid-attempt, the attempt pauses and then continues. Readings already taken are kept, and every reading taken is recorded.
6. **Incomplete attempts.** An attempt without 10 readings from each family when its extended window closes ends without agreement. As in ADR-001, no attempt proceeds on one family.
7. **Outcome.** As in rule 7: 19 of 20 in attempt 1 gives the verdict; otherwise attempt 2 decides; otherwise the call is unresolvable and scored under rule 8.
8. **Records.** Each attempt ends in exactly one chain record: the verdict if it agreed, otherwise an attempt record. If attempt 2 ends without agreement, its record is the unresolvable verdict. Each record carries:
   - the YES, NO and other counts per family;
   - the model names and versions;
   - the evidence hashes;
   - every wait, with UTC start and end and the SHA-256 of its error log.

## Scope and precedence

For calls registered after this record, this record governs wherever it differs from ADR-000 or ADR-001.

- Rule 3 gains the equal-value check (B3).
- Rule 4 gains two record types: decline (A4) and attempt (C8).
- Rule 5 gains one exception: the decline in A1.
- Rules 6, 7 and 8 read with Decisions B and C.
- Calls registered before this record keep the rules in force at their registration, including rule 8 without bounds and rule 7 without a defined wait.

## Consequences

The Record can show its own selection, and no single call or outage can decide its score.

- Declines are counted and, outside LEGAL, readable with their p and b.
- Each result is bounded at about ±4.5951, so no single call can decide a sum.
- A forecast computed as certain registers as 0.01 or 0.99; the computed value stays visible in the record.
- A MISSED caused by the machinery is possible only after 14 days of logged waits.
- Because a spend refusal pauses resolution, the spend gate (2.8) must reserve readings for admission and the Resolver. That is an operating rule, outside this record.
- The operating rule in 2.9 is rewritten to match Decision A when this record takes effect.
